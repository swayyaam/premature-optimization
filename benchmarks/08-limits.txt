Step 8: the rate limit and the in flight limit doing their jobs

Date:    2026-09-04 17:43 IST
Machine: Apple M5, 10 cores, macOS 26.6.2

Both are off or generous by default, because every request in these tests comes
from 127.0.0.1 and a per client rate limit would refuse the load generator
itself. They are shown here with settings low enough to trigger.

--------------------------- rate limit ---------------------------

  RATE_LIMIT=10 ./shortener
  25 requests sent one after another, as fast as curl manages

  302 302 302 302 302 302 302 302 302 302 429 429 429 429 429 429 429 429
  429 429 302 429 429 429 429

  The first ten are the burst. After that the bucket is empty and requests are
  refused with 429 until it refills, which is the single 302 in position 21,
  about a tenth of a second later at ten per second.

  Body of a refused request: "too many requests", with a Retry-After header.

  After waiting a second: 302.

------------------------ in flight limit ------------------------

  MAX_IN_FLIGHT=5 ./shortener
  hey -z 6s -c 50 -disable-redirects http://localhost:8080/seed250000

  Status code distribution:
    [302]	53266 responses
    [503]	432681 responses

  A second identical run, looking at the requests that were let through:
    99% in 0.0014 secs

  Five slots against fifty concurrent clients means most requests are refused.
  The ones that are accepted are served at the same speed as a server with no
  load problem at all, which is the whole point. The work that is admitted is
  done properly and the rest is turned away immediately, rather than everything
  being accepted and everything becoming slow.
